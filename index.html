Publications & Manuscripts

AI-Driven Per-Phase Power Factor and Harmonic Distortion Forecasting with Risk Classification for Modern Distribution Grids with High EV Penetration
Primary Author • Submitted & Under Review (2026)

Summary: Developed a causal, segment-aware machine learning framework using Random Forest and XGBoost to forecast per-phase power factor (PF) and voltage total harmonic distortion (VTHD) 10 minutes ahead[cite: 2].

  • Dataset: High-resolution real-world power quality data (10-second intervals via Fluke 435 Series II) from a high-density e-rickshaw EV charging facility in Bangladesh[cite: 2].
  • Performance: Random Forest achieved top performance for PF across all phases (\(R^2\) up to 0.7195)[cite: 2]. XGBoost led Phase A VTHD forecasting (\(R^2 = 0.7950\)), while Random Forest outperformed on Phases B and C[cite: 2].
  • Key Contribution: Integrated an IEEE-aligned, rule-based operational risk classification mechanism (Good/Acceptable/Poor) to enable proactive grid control, such as automated harmonic filter switching or EV charging delays[cite: 2].
Domain-Informed and Explainable Machine Learning for Transformer Health Index Prediction: A Comparative Study of Two Diagnostic Datasets
Primary Author • Submitted & Under Review (2026)

Summary: Developed a domain-informed, explainable machine learning framework for predicting power transformer Health Index (HI), benchmarked across two distinct public diagnostic datasets (Dataset A: N=730; Dataset B: N=470)[cite: 1].

  • Feature Engineering: Formulated physical degradation metrics, including composite Oil Quality Indices (OQI), non-linear interaction terms (e.g., Acid × Water), physical ratios, and furan-based degree of polymerization estimates[cite: 1].
  • Performance: Tree-based ensembles outperformed linear baselines, with Gradient Boosting achieving \(R^2 = 0.9697\) on Dataset A and \(R^2 = 0.7301\) on Dataset B[cite: 1].
  • Explainability (SHAP): Revealed dataset structural differences—Dataset A was dominated by furan concentrations (cellulose aging)[cite: 1], whereas Dataset B relied on corrosive sulfur (DBDS), dissolved gases, and engineered interaction terms[cite: 1].
  • Key Contribution: Highlighted the risk of predictor-target circularity in ML models when reconstructing legacy scoring formulas versus learning true physical degradation[cite: 1].
